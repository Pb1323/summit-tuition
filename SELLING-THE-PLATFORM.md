## Step 9 — pricing reference (2026-09-11, market-rate sanity check)

Researched comparable UK EdTech/tutoring-sector pricing to sanity-check the "above £2k, no ceiling" floor. **Caveat: none of these are exact comparables** (Summit isn't a per-seat SaaS subscription or a schools-market content licence — it's closer to a one-off/annual platform-plus-content licence to a business, which is a less standardized market with less public pricing) — treat this as directional, not a formula.

- **Atom Learning** (11+ prep platform, closest content comparable) — consumer subscriptions £39.99-£69.99/month per family. For institutional/school-style licensing, per-pupil rates in the region of **£5-£30/pupil/year** are typical for this kind of content-licence arrangement in the UK EdTech market (source: general EdTech institutional-licensing benchmarks, not an Atom-specific published rate — they don't publish B2B pricing). Applied illustratively: a centre with ~150-300 active students (roughly Teachitright's likely scale per the earlier revenue estimate) at even a conservative £10-£20/pupil/year would be **£1,500-£6,000/year** — which brackets the "above £2k" floor sensibly for an ongoing licence rather than a one-off.
- **Institutional EdTech licences generally** — smaller-institution content/platform licences commonly start around **£7,500-£10,000/year** (roughly $10k) as a base tier before per-seat scaling, per general EdTech-institutional-pricing benchmarks (not tutoring-specific, but the closest published anchor for "a business buying a whole platform/content package" rather than per-seat consumer pricing).
- **Tutoring-sector SaaS tooling (adjacent, not content)** — TutorCruncher (scheduling/CRM, not content) charges from £25/month base, **+£40/month per additional branch/centre** — useful as a per-centre pricing *mechanism* reference even though it's a different product category: a per-centre add-on model (rather than a single flat fee) may be a cleaner way to structure a multi-centre Teachitright deal than one lump sum, and gives a concrete lever if he pushes back on a flat number ("£X per centre" scales naturally with however many of his 3 centres actually use it).
- **Replacement-cost framing (already the core pitch, restated for scale)** — the "£15-30k of content-writing+dev work, 6+ months" estimate underlying the original ~£10k lump-sum framing was calibrated for a solo/small tutor buyer. Teachitright is a substantially larger operation (est. £700k-£1.8m/yr revenue per prior research) — for a business at that scale, £15-30k of avoided build cost is a rounding error, not a meaningful number to anchor on. A multi-centre licence priced in the low-to-mid **£5,000-£15,000/year** range (or a larger one-off, e.g. £8,000-£20,000, if Chris prefers a lump sum over a recurring licence) would still comfortably clear the "above £2k, no ceiling" floor while sitting inside what the per-pupil/institutional-licence comparables above suggest is normal for a business this size — worth having a number in that range in mind before the call, even though the plan is still to let him ask first.

**Bottom line for the call:** the £2k floor was set assuming a small solo-tutor buyer; Teachitright's real scale means the actual number discussed is very likely to land well above that floor once scope is clear — the comparables above support pricing multi-centre access in the low thousands per year (or a larger one-off), not just barely over £2k.
