SPaG, Comprehension & Maths — QE Barnet Recap

Exam in 2 days (QE Barnet — English & Maths papers, no VR). Use as a rapid-fire "have we covered this" checklist, not a worksheet.

1 · SPaG (Spelling, Punctuation & Grammar)

Spelling

  • Homophones (there/their/they're, its/it's, your/you're)
  • Common 11+ trap words (necessary, definitely, separate, accommodate)
  • Plurals of tricky nouns (-ies, -ves, irregular)
  • Prefixes/suffixes changing meaning (un-, dis-, -able, -ible)

Punctuation

  • Apostrophes: contraction vs. possession, joint possession
  • Comma splices — spot and fix
  • Semicolon vs. comma vs. full stop
  • Quotation marks around reported/direct speech
  • Hyphenation in compound modifiers

Grammar

  • Subject–verb agreement (tricky collective nouns)
  • Tense consistency across a passage
  • Correct relative pronoun (who/which/that)
  • "Find the mistake" segment format — practise under time

Cloze C·L·O·Z·E

Cloze = fill-the-gap passage. One missing word per numbered blank.
  • Read the WHOLE sentence (before + after the gap) before choosing
  • Check the word fits grammatically, not just sense
  • Watch for connective/linking words (although, whereas, since)
  • Re-read the finished sentence to confirm it flows

2 · Comprehension

Core technique

  • Skim first for overall gist, then scan for detail per question
  • Underline key nouns/names as you read
  • Answer using the passage's own evidence, not outside knowledge
  • Watch for "NOT true / NOT mentioned" questions — read every option
  • Retrieval questions: find the exact matching detail, watch for near-identical decoys

Inference techniques

  • "What does this suggest about X?" → find evidence, don't guess
  • Combine clues from more than one paragraph
  • Character/author "voice" questions — what word would they use?
  • Don't over-read — the safest inference is the one closest to the text

Literary techniques to recognise

  • Simile / Metaphor
  • Personification
  • Alliteration / Onomatopoeia
  • Imagery (visual, sound, touch)
  • Hyperbole
  • Foreshadowing
  • Repetition for emphasis

3 · Three Layers — Character & Setting

Character (3 layers)

  • Physical — appearance, what we see
  • Emotional — feelings, inner thoughts
  • Behavioural — actions, what they do/say

Setting (3 layers)

  • Physical — what's there, concrete detail
  • Sensory / Imagery — sounds, smells, textures
  • Atmosphere / Mood — how it makes the reader feel

Show, Not Tell

  • Replace a flat statement ("she was sad") with action/sensory detail (her hands trembled, she looked away)
  • Use one character/setting layer at a time to build up description, don't dump all three at once

4 · Maths — Quick Recap

  • Arithmetic: BIDMAS, negative numbers, rounding
  • Fractions/decimals/percentages: converting between, percentage increase/decrease
  • Ratio & proportion: simplifying, sharing in a ratio
  • Algebra: solving simple equations, substitution
  • Geometry: angles (point/triangle), area/perimeter
  • Statistics: mean/median/range, reading charts
