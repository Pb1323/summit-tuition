The Beekeeper's Daughter

1. Every June, when the lavender began to bloom along the lower field, Ines helped her mother lift the heavy hive frames, checking each one for the telltale glisten of new honey.

2. Her mother moved among the bees without gloves, humming under her breath, and claimed the bees could sense a nervous hand from a steady one. Ines was not sure she believed this, but she had never been stung while humming too.

3. This year, though, one hive had fallen silent. No bees streamed from its entrance, and the frames inside were empty of the restless, golden motion Ines had grown up with.

4. Her mother knelt beside the quiet hive for a long time, turning a single dead bee over in her palm as though it might explain itself if she waited long enough.

5. "A hive can die of many things," she said eventually, "but mostly it dies of being alone. Bees only thrive when there are enough of them to keep each other warm."

6. Ines thought about that sentence for the rest of the summer, long after the other hives had filled with honey again, wondering how many things beyond beekeeping might be true in the same way.
