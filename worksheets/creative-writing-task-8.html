Descriptive Writing — New Format

Describe the Scene

Difficult Practice

No plot, no characters, no dialogue this time — just the place itself, built entirely out of the words you choose.

Name:  Date set:  Due: 

Choose ONE of the following

1. Describe a busy railway station at the exact moment the last train of the night pulls in.
2. Describe an abandoned playground the morning after a storm.

This is a different skill from a story. Don't invent a plot or name any characters — you're painting a picture with words, not telling us what happens. A reader should be able to picture the place clearly and feel something about it, without anyone in it doing anything.

Technique required

Build the picture

Appeal to at least 4 of the 5 senses — not just what it looks like.
2 similes and 1 metaphor, each comparing to something that actually suits the mood of the scene.
1 example of personification — give something non-living a human quality or action.
Vary your sentence lengths — include at least one very short sentence (one or two words) for impact.
2 semicolons and 1 colon, used naturally.
Shape: start wide (the whole scene) → zoom into 2–3 specific details → end on one lasting image or feeling.

Your writing

Checklist — go over it again

  1. Read it all out loud — does it actually sound like a real place?
  2. No clichés — check for overused phrases ("as quiet as a mouse," "the calm before the storm") and replace with your own image.
  3. Every simile or metaphor compares to something that fits the mood — not just something that rhymes or sounds clever.
  4. Was / were — check it matches a singular or plural subject.
  5. Every sentence has a verb, except your deliberate one- or two-word sentences.
  6. No word repeated in the same sentence or the one right after it — swap for a synonym.
  7. Ending leaves one lasting image — don't summarise ("Overall, the station was busy") or trail off.
