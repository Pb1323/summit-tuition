Practice Set A

1Word class

In the sentence "The excited dog barked loudly," which word is the adjective?

2Verb tense

Choose the correct verb to complete the sentence: "By the time we arrived, the film already ___."

A had startedB has startedC startsD was starting
3Pronoun

Choose the correct pronoun: "Neither Sam nor his sisters had brought ___ umbrella."

A hisB herC theirD its
4Subject-verb agreement

Which sentence is correct?

A The team of players were tired.B The team of players was tired.
5Common error

Rewrite this sentence, correcting the error: "There was less children at the party than last year."

6Sentence type

What type of sentence is this: "Close the door quietly."?

A StatementB QuestionC CommandD Exclamation
7Adverbs

Rewrite this sentence, changing the underlined adjective into an adverb: "She spoke in a careful way." → "She spoke ___."

8Conjunctions

Choose the connective that best completes the sentence: "We left early ___ we wanted to avoid the traffic."

A althoughB becauseC unlessD whereas
9Sentence structure

Is this sentence simple, compound, or complex? "Although it was raining, the match went ahead."

10Active / passive

Rewrite this sentence in the passive voice: "The chef prepared the meal."

Answer Key & Explanations — Set A

1.
"Excited" is the adjective.
It describes the noun "dog." "Loudly" describes the verb "barked," so that's an adverb, not an adjective.
2.
Correct answer: A — had started
The film finished happening before we arrived, so it needs the past perfect ("had started") to show one past action happened before another past action.
3.
Correct answer: C — their
With "neither...nor," the pronoun agrees with the noun closest to it — here that's "his sisters" (plural), so "their" is needed, not the singular "his."
4.
Correct answer: B — The team of players was tired.
"Team" is the actual subject of the sentence (singular), not "players" — so the verb should be "was," not "were."
5.
There were fewer children at the party than last year.
"Fewer" is used for things you can count (children), and "less" is used for amounts you can't count (like water or time). "Was" also needs to become "were" to agree with the plural "children."
6.
Correct answer: C — Command
It's giving an instruction (an imperative), not stating a fact, asking something, or showing strong feeling.
7.
She spoke carefully.
Adverbs describing how something is done usually add "-ly" to the adjective — careful becomes carefully.
8.
Correct answer: B — because
The second half of the sentence gives the reason for the first half, so it needs a connective that shows cause, not contrast ("although," "whereas") or condition ("unless").
9.
Complex sentence.
It has a main clause ("the match went ahead") and a subordinate clause ("Although it was raining") that can't stand alone as a full sentence — that combination makes it complex.
10.
The meal was prepared by the chef.
In the passive voice, the thing being acted upon (the meal) becomes the subject of the sentence, and "by the chef" shows who did the action.

4Subject-verb agreement

Which sentence is correct?

A Neither of the answers were right.B Neither of the answers was right.
5Common error

Rewrite this sentence, correcting the error: "She done her homework before dinner."

6Sentence type

What type of sentence is this: "What an incredible goal that was!"?

A StatementB QuestionC CommandD Exclamation
7Adverbs

Rewrite this sentence, changing the underlined adjective into an adverb: "He gave a quick reply." → "He replied ___."

8Conjunctions

Choose the connective that best completes the sentence: "I enjoy swimming, ___ my brother prefers football."

A becauseB whereasC sinceD so
9Sentence structure

Is this sentence simple, compound, or complex? "The sun set, and the sky turned orange."

10Active / passive

Rewrite this sentence in the passive voice: "The gardener watered the plants."

Answer Key & Explanations — Set B (tutor key)

1.
"Peacefully" is the adverb.
It describes how the hikers rested (the verb). "Tired" is the adjective describing "hikers."
2.
Correct answer: B — are going
"Tomorrow" signals a planned future action, which in everyday English is usually expressed with "am/is/are going to," not the simple present or past tense.
3.
Correct answer: B — her
"Each" is singular, and here it clearly refers to "girls," so the singular feminine "her" is correct rather than the plural "their."
4.
Correct answer: B — Neither of the answers was right.
"Neither" is singular even though it's followed by a plural noun phrase ("of the answers") — the verb should agree with "neither," not "answers."
5.
She did her homework before dinner.
"Done" needs a helping verb ("had done" or "has done") to work correctly — on its own as the main verb of the sentence, the correct past tense form is "did."
6.
Correct answer: D — Exclamation
It expresses strong feeling and starts with "What," a classic exclamation-sentence opener, ending in an exclamation mark.
7.
He replied quickly.
Quick becomes quickly by adding "-ly," following the same pattern as question 7 in Set A.
8.
Correct answer: B — whereas
The sentence is contrasting two different preferences, so it needs a contrasting connective — "because," "since" and "so" all suggest cause and effect, which isn't what's happening here.
9.
Compound sentence.
It joins two complete sentences ("The sun set" and "the sky turned orange") with "and" — both halves could stand alone, which is what makes it compound rather than complex.
10.
The plants were watered by the gardener.
The thing receiving the action (the plants) becomes the subject, and "by the gardener" shows who did it — same pattern as Set A Q10.
