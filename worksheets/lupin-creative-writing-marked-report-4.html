Annotated extract

Danger.

It surrounded me.

None of these thoughts had occurred to me earlier today. Going back to this morning, I remembered that my friend Tom and I were going to climb an inactive volcano; however, I was having a feeling something terrible was going to happen. Before we checked that we had everything1: food, water, climbing rope, hiking sticks and cameras.

An hour later, we were hiking until we reached 5000m and decided to have a break. "How much longer do you think we need to climb to the top?" he would ask me every once in a while, just as we were going to continue, lava started to surround me like a bad smell until I was on an island of rock surrounded by a sea of lava.

"Help!" I shouted but Tom could not do anything. He started making a sort of a bridge2 by putting large rocks, but he barely made it any closer because it was so deep. Instead of making him place more, I told him I was going to try jump3.

Was it even possible?

He took some steps back while I prepared myself. Annoyingly, when I looked at the lava it urged me to leap. It felt like jumping off a cliff. Vroom! Rumble! was the sound I heard after jumping successfully across to the other side. Behind me the rocks were melted in the lava...4

Corrections

1
"Before we checked" doesn't say before what — it reads like the connecting word got left out.→"Before we set off, we checked that we had everything"
2
"A sort of a bridge" doubles up the word "a" — only one is needed.→"a sort of bridge"
3
"Try jump" is missing the infinitive "to" — you always try to do something.→"I was going to try to jump"
4
The piece stops on "..." right after the jump instead of a real final sentence — the same issue as your last report.→"...melted into the lava behind me, and for the first time all day, I let myself breathe."

Strengths

A structural idea, planned in advance
  • Opening on "Danger. It surrounded me." before flashing back to the morning is a proper in medias res opening — you're starting at the most dramatic point and earning the backstory afterwards, not just telling events in order.
  • Calling the volcano "inactive" in paragraph one is dramatic irony done on purpose — you set the word up so it could be broken later, which is exactly the kind of planning that separates a strong piece from a random one.
  • "Was it even possible?" standing alone as its own paragraph is the same one-line-tension trick as "Half an hour later:" from your last piece — good to see you reusing a technique deliberately rather than by accident.
Editing as you go
  • Multiple live corrections visible on your own page (fixing "was" to "were," catching and rewriting a misspelling mid-word) — exactly the habit that will eventually catch things like "try jump" too.
  • "Lava started to surround me like a bad smell" is a genuinely unusual simile — an unexpected sense (smell) for a visual threat (lava), which is more memorable than the obvious comparison.
  • "Vroom! Rumble!" as its own short burst of onomatopoeia gives the jump real sound rather than just describing the action.

Next steps

Focus for next time: (1) the ending, again — plan your last sentence before you start writing so you always have somewhere to land, rather than running out of time; (2) read the very first sentence of each paragraph back and check it actually says what happened before it, not just what happened next ("Before we checked" needs to say before what); (3) watch for missing small linking words like "to" after "try" — these are easy to skip when writing fast and easy to catch on a slow read-back.
