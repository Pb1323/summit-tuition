1 · Copy This Down — The Three Layers of Setting

Definition

Setting is where and when a story happens — but in comprehension, setting is never just background. Writers choose setting details on purpose, to create a mood (a feeling) and to hint at what a character is thinking or what is about to happen.

The Technique — Read setting in 3 layers, every time

  1. Layer 1 — Physical detail: What is literally there? (place, weather, time of day, objects)
  2. Layer 2 — Sensory language: What can be seen, heard, smelt, or felt? (the actual words used)
  3. Layer 3 — Mood/effect: What feeling does Layer 1 + Layer 2 create — and why did the writer pick these details and not others?

Key words to know: atmosphere (the overall feeling of a place), imagery (descriptive language that appeals to the senses), pathetic fallacy (giving weather/nature human feelings, e.g. "an angry sky").

2 · Worked Example

"The corridor was narrow and dim, the single bulb flickering above them. Their footsteps echoed unnaturally loud on the bare floorboards."

Layer 1 narrow, dim corridor, one flickering bulb, bare floorboards

Layer 2 "flickering" (sight), "echoed unnaturally loud" (sound)

Layer 3 Cramped space + unreliable light + loud noise in a place that should be quiet = tense, uneasy mood. The writer wants the reader to feel nervous, the same way the characters probably do.

3 · Practice Passage

1The tide had gone out further than Mara had ever seen it, leaving behind a stretch of grey sand pitted with pools and strung with dark ribbons of seaweed. Gulls circled overhead, calling to each other in short, sharp bursts, as if arguing over something only they could see.

2She had come down to the beach hoping the walk would clear her head before the exam results were posted, but the emptiness of the place did the opposite. No other footprints marked the sand. The wind kept dropping and rising again, tugging at her coat, and somewhere out past the pools a buoy bell rang, slow and irregular, like it couldn't decide whether to warn anyone or not.

1. Using Layer 1, list two physical details of the setting.
2. Using Layer 2, pick one piece of sensory language from the passage and say which sense it appeals to.
3. Using Layer 3, what mood does this setting create? Use one piece of evidence to support your answer.
4. "No other footprints marked the sand." Why might the writer have included this detail about Mara's setting, given how she is feeling?
5. The buoy bell is described as ringing "like it couldn't decide whether to warn anyone or not." What does this suggest about Mara's own state of mind?
Apply this every time: Whenever a comprehension question mentions "setting," "atmosphere," or "how the writer creates a feeling of ___," always run the 3 layers — Physical → Sensory → Mood. Never just say what a setting looks like; always explain the feeling it creates and point to the exact word or phrase that creates it.

Marking Notes (for the tutor)

  1. Q1: Any two of: grey sand, pools, seaweed, gulls, wind, empty beach.
  2. Q2: E.g. "calling in short, sharp bursts" (sound); "dark ribbons of seaweed" (sight). Accept any correctly matched sense.
  3. Q3: Look for: lonely / uneasy / unsettled mood, supported by evidence like the empty beach, no footprints, or the irregular bell.
  4. Q4: Should link the absence of footprints to Mara's isolation/anxiety before her results — the empty setting mirrors her emotional state.
  5. Q5: Should link the bell's uncertainty to Mara's own uncertainty/anxiety about her exam results — setting reflecting internal feeling, not just literal description.
