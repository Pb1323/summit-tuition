The Ice Cave

It was a choice between life and death.

Nobody had ever thought that this would ever happen. Earlier, my friends and I were planning to explore this dark, musty cave, which was in an icy mountain. We checked that we had everything: ice picks, helnets1, flashlights and food.

We started off by climbing using the metal ice piks2 until we reached the mouth off3 the mysterious cave. Inside4, there were two paths, so we split into two groups of two. Moments later, one of our friends, Bob, was suddenly yelling his5 like a chicken with its head off. "Help!" he screamed while we came darting from the other path.

He was hanging on by a strand of hair.

In front of us there was a large, deep hole, where he was gripping an ice pick. Based off6 the ice, we could tell it was going to break in any minute7. Fortunately we could reach his hips, where we strapped a rope.

"3... 2... 1... Pull!" I chanted, and after thirty tiresome minutes which felt like a day, we managed to pull him out. Suddenly a wall of ice covered the exit just as we were leaving. We were trapped...

Corrections

  • 1Spelling."helmets"
  • 2Spelling — same word as above ("picks"), correct earlier in the piece, misspelled here."picks"
  • 3Wrong preposition — "the mouth off the cave" should be "of.""...the mouth of the mysterious cave."
  • 4Missing full stop — "cave inside" runs two ideas together as one sentence."...mysterious cave. Inside, there were two paths..."
  • 5Missing word — the joke needs "head off" before "like a chicken", or it just reads as "yelling his... like a chicken.""...was suddenly yelling his head off, like a chicken with its head off."
  • 6Wrong preposition again — "based on," not "based off." This is the same mistake as Correction 3, worth remembering as one rule, not two separate slips."Based on the ice, we could tell..."
  • 7Wrong preposition — "in any minute" isn't standard English."...it was going to break at any moment."

Model Version — Same Story, Full Marks

Same plot, same characters — written to a 25/25 standard

It was a choice between life and death.

Nobody had ever imagined that today would be the day everything went wrong. Earlier, my friends and I had planned to explore a dark, musty cave hidden deep in an icy mountain range. We checked our kit twice: ice picks, helmets, flashlights, and enough food to last the day.

We began the climb, driving our picks into the frozen rock until we finally reached the cave's mouth. Inside, the tunnel split into two paths, so we split into two groups of two, agreeing to meet back at the entrance within the hour.

Barely five minutes in, a scream tore through the tunnel. "Help!" Bob's voice echoed off the ice walls, high and frantic, like a chicken with its head cut off. We sprinted back down our own path, boots skidding on the frozen ground, hearts hammering.

He was hanging on by a strand of hair.

In front of us gaped a wide, bottomless-looking hole, and Bob dangled over it, his knuckles white around an ice pick wedged into the wall. Thin cracks were already spidering out from where the pick met the ice — it would not hold much longer. Without a word, one of us unclipped the rope and looped it under his arms while the rest of us gripped his wrists.

"Three! Two! One! Pull!" I roared, and we heaved together, boots braced against the rock. Thirty exhausting minutes later — minutes that felt like hours — we finally dragged him onto solid ground, shaking and gasping, but alive.

We did not have time to celebrate. A deafening crack echoed through the cave, and a wall of ice sealed the exit shut behind us. We looked at each other in silence, torches trembling in our hands. We were trapped — but we were alive, and as long as we were alive, we still had a way out to find.

What makes this version full marks: every sentence is technically clean (no missing words, no wrong prepositions), it actually finishes (the last line resolves the danger into a new, forward-looking problem instead of trailing off), and it slows down at the one moment that matters — the rescue — with real sensory detail (cracking ice, white knuckles, boots braced) instead of rushing past it in one sentence.

Next Steps

Focus for next time — 1) Finish the story properly — three pieces in a row have stopped on "...", so plan your last line before you start writing, not after. 2) Slow the rescue down: add a sentence or two of what it looked, sounded or felt like as the ice cracked, instead of jumping straight to "we managed to pull him out." 3) Read your last sentence back before moving on — that's how you'll catch missing words like in Correction 2.
