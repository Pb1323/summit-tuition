Why This Plan Looks Different

Anagha hasn't sat an NVR paper yet, so unlike the Maths and English plans, there's no marked report to target weak topics from. This plan starts with a genuine baseline assessment in week 1, then adjusts around whatever it shows — the specific target percentage and weak pattern types below will get set after that first attempt, not guessed in advance.

Worth flagging honestly: NVR doesn't yet have a dedicated Study Notes strand on the platform the way Maths and English do (it's a known content gap, being worked on). For now, the pattern-type coaching below is tutor-led — live explanation plus printed practice questions — rather than self-study notes pages.

Week By Week

1

Baseline Assessment

Week 1
  • Complete Non-Verbal Reasoning Pattern Lab (20 questions / 24 marks, /dashboard → unlocked mocks) cold, with no prior coaching or prep

The point of this week is honest data, not a good score — going in cold shows which of the classic NVR question types (sequences, matrices, rotations, reflections, nets, analogies, odd-one-out, code-breaking) she already handles instinctively and which need real teaching. Resist the urge to prep beforehand; a coached first attempt would hide exactly what we need to see.

Sequences Matrices Rotations Reflections Nets / 3D Analogies Odd-One-Out Code-Breaking
2

Targeted Coaching on Weakest Types

Week 2
  • Tutor-led session on whichever 2-3 pattern types the baseline showed weakest
  • Fresh practice questions pulled by pattern type (Admin → Homework Generator, subject: NVR)

For each weak type: tutor demonstrates the underlying rule first (e.g. "each shape rotates 90° clockwise and gains one dot"), then Anagha talks through 3-4 questions out loud before answering, not just after getting one wrong. This is the part that can't come from notes alone — NVR rules need to be seen worked through live.

3

Independent Practice, Same Weak Types

Week 3
  • Fresh questions on the same 2-3 pattern types from week 2, this time answered independently first
  • Tutor checks by asking her to explain the rule out loud before revealing whether she got it right

Goal: she should be able to state the rule confidently before checking the answer, not reverse-engineer it from being told she's wrong. If she's still guessing rather than reasoning by the end of this week, week 2's coaching repeats on those specific types before moving on.

4

Second Full Assessment

Week 4
  • Retake a full NVR paper covering all pattern types, not just the weak ones from week 1

How this decides what's next: if the retake shows solid improvement on the week-1 weak spots and a comfortable overall score, she's ready to try a genuinely harder paper next month. If the weak spots are still weak, this plan repeats for another 4 weeks on the same pattern types before introducing anything harder — there's no value pushing to a tougher paper before the fundamentals are solid.
