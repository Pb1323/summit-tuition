Source A: Newspaper report

Cedarbrook Trail Reopens After Storm Repairs

1Cedarbrook Wildlife Reserve's main circular trail reopened to the public on Saturday, three weeks after a spring storm brought down branches across several sections of the path. Rangers closed the whole 3.5 km loop for safety while the damage was assessed.

2The worst damage was at Old Oak Bridge, the wooden crossing over Cedar Beck near the halfway point of the loop, where a falling branch cracked two of the main support planks. A team of twelve volunteers joined the reserve's three rangers over a single weekend to replace the damaged timber and reinforce the handrail.

3Since reopening, visitor numbers have already begun climbing back towards their usual spring level, helped by clear weather and a new set of trail signs installed during the closure.

4Head Ranger Elena Okafor said the repair work had also given the team a chance to protect the reserve's breeding herons early. 'We've used the closure to put up a temporary screen around Heron Hide,' she explained. 'The birds are nesting a little earlier than usual this year, so we're keeping that section quieter until the chicks fledge.'

5The reserve is marking the reopening with a free guided walk starting from the Visitor Centre on the last Saturday of the month, tracing the full loop past Otter Pond, the repaired bridge and the meadow picnic area. Places are limited and must be booked in advance at the Visitor Centre.

6Okafor thanked everyone who took part in the repair weekend. 'Cedarbrook only stays open because people give up their Saturdays for it,' she said. 'We're grateful, and we're glad to have the whole loop open again before the busy summer season begins.'

Trail Notice — Heron Hide

Posted by the Cedarbrook Ranger Team · updated this week

Heron Hide remains open, but a temporary screen has been placed along the boardwalk to protect nesting herons. Please keep noise to a minimum and do not remove or look behind the screen.

The screen will stay in place until the chicks fledge, expected by early summer. Thank you for helping to give this year's herons a quiet start.
